# SPORTS HUB PLATFORM — SPORTS SAFETY, FIRST-AID & RECOVERY KNOWLEDGE BASE

## SECTION 1: DYNAMIC WARM-UP GUIDELINES (RAMP PROTOCOL)
Before any high-intensity training or tournament match (badminton, football, tennis, basketball, running), athletes should complete a 10-to-15-minute structured dynamic warm-up rather than prolonged static stretching:
1. RAISE (3-4 minutes): Elevate core body temperature, heart rate, and blood flow with light jogging, high knees, butt kicks, side shuffles, and skipping.
2. ACTIVATE & MOBILIZE (4-5 minutes):
   - Dynamic lunges with thoracic rotation ("World's Greatest Stretch") — 5 reps per side.
   - Glute bridges and lateral mini-band monster walks — 10 reps.
   - Walking leg swings (front-to-back and side-to-side) — 10 reps per leg.
   - Scapular push-ups and controlled arm circles for overhead racquet/court sports.
3. POTENTIATE (3-4 minutes):
   - Progressive build-up sprints at 60%, 75%, and 90% effort.
   - Deceleration and plant-and-cut footwork drills focusing on soft knee landings ("knee over toe", avoiding inward knee valgus collapse).

## SECTION 2: ANKLE SPRAIN FIRST-AID (RICE & PEACE AND LOVE PROTOCOLS)
Lateral ankle sprains (inversion injuries affecting the ATFL and CFL ligaments) are the most common acute injury in court and field sports.

### Immediate First-Aid: The RICE Protocol (First 24-48 Hours)
- REST: Stop playing immediately. Protect the ankle from further weight-bearing stress that causes sharp pain.
- ICE: Apply an ice pack wrapped in a damp towel for 10-15 minutes every 2-3 hours during the first 24-48 hours to manage acute pain and excessive swelling. Never apply ice directly to bare skin.
- COMPRESSION: Wrap the ankle snugly with an elastic compression bandage (starting from the toes/midfoot moving upward) to limit swelling. Ensure toes remain warm and pink with normal circulation.
- ELEVATION: Elevate the injured ankle above the level of the heart on pillows while resting to promote fluid drainage.

### Sub-Acute Recovery: The PEACE & LOVE Progression (After 48-72 Hours)
- PROTECTION & OPTIMAL LOADING: Transition from rest to pain-free gentle weight-bearing and ankle range-of-motion ("ankle alphabet" tracing A-Z with the big toe).
- VASCULARISATION & EXERCISE: Perform pain-free stationary cycling, 4-way resistance band ankle strengthening (eversion and dorsiflexion focus), and single-leg proprioceptive balance drills.

### Ottawa Ankle Rules (When to Seek an X-Ray Immediately)
Always consult a physician and obtain an X-ray if there is ankle/midfoot pain accompanied by:
1. Bone tenderness along the posterior edge or tip of the lateral or medial malleolus (bottom 6 cm of the shin/fibula bones).
2. Bone tenderness at the base of the 5th metatarsal or navicular bone.
3. Inability to bear weight for 4 steps both immediately after the injury and during examination.

## SECTION 3: MUSCLE STRAINS, KNEE/ACL PROTECTION & OVERUSE CARE
- HAMSTRING STRAINS: Avoid ballistic stretching during the first 5 days. Progress to eccentric loading (Askling Extender, Diver, Glider, and Nordic Hamstring Curls) once daily movements are pain-free.
- ACL PREVENTION: Land softly with at least 30 degrees of knee and hip flexion; keep knees aligned over toes and avoid single-leg locked-knee pivoting.
- TENNIS ELBOW & SHOULDER IMPINGEMENT: Use isometric wrist extension holds (45 seconds x 5 reps) for tendon pain relief, followed by slow eccentric wrist extensions and scapular Y-T-W strengthening.
- SHIN SPLINTS: Differentiate diffuse posteromedial shin soreness from pinpoint bone tenderness (possible stress fracture—stop running and see a doctor).

---

## SECTION 4: RECOVERY TIPS, HYDRATION & CONCUSSION RED FLAGS
- SLEEP & NUTRITION: Aim for 8-9 hours of sleep per night. Consume 20-30g of high-quality protein and complex carbohydrates within 45-60 minutes post-match to repair muscle micro-trauma and replenish glycogen.
- HYDRATION: Drink 400-600 mL of water 2 hours before play, and 150-250 mL every 15-20 minutes during play. For sessions over 60 minutes in heat, use an electrolyte drink (400-700 mg/L sodium).
- RETURN-TO-PLAY BENCHMARKS: Achieve full pain-free range of motion, zero joint swelling, and >=90% Limb Symmetry Index (LSI) on strength and single-leg hop tests before returning to full tournament competition.
- CONCUSSION RED FLAGS (MEDICAL EMERGENCY): After any head impact, remove the athlete from play immediately ("If in doubt, sit them out"). Seek emergency medical care for loss of consciousness, worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion, slurred speech, unequal pupils, or limb weakness/numbness.
