# Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) Injury Prevention (FIFA 11+ & PEP)

## High-Risk Biomechanics
Over 70% of ACL injuries in football, basketball, and court sports are non-contact, occurring during single-leg landing, abrupt deceleration, or pivoting when the knee collapses inward (**dynamic knee valgus**) with an overly upright trunk and extended hip/knee.

## Key Biomechanical Cues for Athletes
- **"Knee Over Toe":** Prevent the knee from caving medially during squats, lunges, and jump landings.
- **Soft, Quiet Landings:** Land on the balls of the feet rolling to midfoot with at least 30° of knee and hip flexion to absorb ground reaction forces through the glutes and hamstrings.
- **Decelerate with Multiple Steps:** Avoid planting a single locked leg when cutting at high speed; use choppy deceleration steps to lower your center of mass.

## Evidence-Based ACL Prevention Routine (Perform 2–3x Weekly)
1. **Neuromuscular Control:** Single-leg squats in front of a mirror checking hip-knee-ankle alignment (3 x 10/side).
2. **Posterior Chain Ratio:** Nordic hamstring curls and single-leg hip thrusts to ensure hamstring-to-quadriceps strength ratio exceeds 65%.
3. **Plyometric Landing Mechanics:** Box drop-jumps focusing on 2-second stick landings without valgus collapse (3 x 8 reps).
4. **Core & Trunk Stability:** Copenhagen adductor planks and side planks with hip abduction.

## Warning Signs of Acute ACL Tear
Audible/felt "pop", rapid joint effusion (hemarthrosis swelling within 2–6 hours), and a feeling of the knee "giving way". Always stop play immediately and consult an orthopedic sports physician.
