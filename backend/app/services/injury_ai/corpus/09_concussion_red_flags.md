# Sports Concussion Recognition, Red Flags & Return-to-Play Protocol

## IMPORTANT MEDICAL DISCLAIMER
**Suspected concussion is a medical priority. Always remove the athlete from play immediately ("If in doubt, sit them out") and require evaluation by a licensed physician or emergency medical professional.** Never allow same-day return to play after a suspected concussion.

## Common Concussion Symptoms
A concussion can result from a direct blow to the head, face, neck, or an impulsive force transmitted from a body collision:
- Headache, pressure in head, dizziness, balance problems, or blurred/double vision.
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia) or noise (phonophobia).
- Feeling slowed down, "in a fog," confusion, amnesia, or difficulty concentrating.

## EMERGENCY RED FLAGS (Call Emergency Services / Go to ER Immediately)
Seek emergency hospital care immediately if the athlete exhibits ANY of the following:
1. Loss of consciousness (even brief) or deteriorating alertness/drowsiness.
2. Severe or progressively worsening headache.
3. Repeated vomiting or severe nausea.
4. Seizures, convulsions, or abnormal posturing.
5. Slurred speech, unequal pupil size, double vision, or numbness/weakness/tingling in arms or legs.
6. Neck pain or midline cervical spine tenderness.

## Graduated 6-Stage Return-to-Play (Minimum 24 Hours Per Stage, Under Doctor Supervision)
1. Symptom-limited daily activities (after 24–48h initial relative physical/cognitive rest).
2. Light aerobic exercise (walking or stationary bike at <70% max HR, no resistance training).
3. Sport-specific individual drills (running/footwork, zero head impact risk).
4. Non-contact training drills and progressive resistance training.
5. Full-contact practice **only after written medical clearance by a physician**.
6. Return to competitive match play.
