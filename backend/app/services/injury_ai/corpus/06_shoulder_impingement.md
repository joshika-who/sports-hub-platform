# Shoulder Impingement & Rotator Cuff Care for Overhead Athletes

## What Is Subacromial Pain / Shoulder Impingement?
In racquet sports (badminton, tennis), swimming, and volleyball, repetitive overhead smashing and serving can narrow the subacromial space, irritating the supraspinatus tendon and subacromial bursa. It is frequently driven by scapular dyskinesis (poor shoulder blade upward rotation) and posterior capsule tightness (GIRD – Glenohumeral Internal Rotation Deficit).

## Symptoms
- Painful arc during shoulder abduction between 60° and 120°.
- Anterior or lateral shoulder ache during overhead serves, smashes, or reaching behind the back.
- Night pain when lying directly on the affected shoulder.

## Corrective Rehabilitation & Prehab Exercises
1. **Scapular Retraction & Lower Trapezius Activation:** Prone Y-T-W raises and wall slides with liftoff (3 sets x 12 reps).
2. **Rotator Cuff External Rotation:** Side-lying dumbbell external rotation (towel roll tucked under elbow) and resistance band face pulls (3 sets x 15 reps).
3. **Serratus Anterior Strengthening:** Push-up plus and dynamic hug with resistance band.
4. **Thoracic Spine Mobility:** Foam roller thoracic extensions and open-book rotations to restore overhead reach without lumbar hyperextension.

## Load Modification
Temporarily reduce overhead smash/serve volume by 40–50%, verify grip size and racquet string tension, and consult a sports physiotherapist if weakness in external rotation or drop-arm signs are present.
