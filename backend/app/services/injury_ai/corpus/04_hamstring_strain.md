# Hamstring Strain Injury: Rehabilitation & Nordic Prevention

## Pathophysiology
Hamstring strains most commonly affect the biceps femoris long head during the terminal swing phase of high-speed sprinting, when the muscle contracts eccentrically while lengthening across both the hip and knee joints.

## Clinical Signs & Red Flags
- Sudden sharp posterior thigh pain ("pop" or snapping sensation) during sprinting or high kicking.
- Focal tenderness on palpation and pain with resisted knee flexion or passive straight-leg raise.
- **Red Flag:** Proximal ischial tuberosity (buttock crease) pain with bruising and severe weakness may indicate an avulsion tear—consult an orthopedic specialist promptly.

## Rehabilitation Phases (Askling L-Protocol)
1. **Acute Phase (Days 1–5):** Pain-free isometric holds, gentle walking, avoiding aggressive ballistic stretching.
2. **Lengthening / Eccentric Phase:**
   - **The Extender:** Supine hip flexed at 90°, perform slow knee extensions to the point just before discomfort (2 sets x 12 reps twice daily).
   - **The Diver:** Single-leg Romanian deadlift (hip hinge) focusing on pelvic control and hamstring lengthening.
   - **The Glider:** Controlled split-stance eccentric sliding exercises.
3. **High-Speed Running Progression:** Gradual exposure to 50%, 70%, 85%, and 100% sprint velocities before full match clearance.

## Prevention Gold Standard
Incorporating **Nordic Hamstring Curls** (2 sessions/week, 3 sets of 6–8 reps) reduces hamstring injury incidence by 51% across field and court athletes.
