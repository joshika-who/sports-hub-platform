# Lateral & Medial Ankle Sprain: Assessment, Care & Prevention

## Mechanism of Injury
Approximately 85% of ankle sprains are inversion injuries affecting the lateral ligament complex—primarily the Anterior Talofibular Ligament (ATFL) and Calcaneofibular Ligament (CFL)—occurring during awkward landings or rapid cutting in basketball, football, tennis, and badminton.

## Ottawa Ankle Rules (When to Get an X-Ray Immediately)
Seek urgent medical evaluation and radiographic imaging if there is pain in the malleolar/midfoot zone accompanied by ANY of the following:
1. Bone tenderness along the posterior edge or tip of the lateral or medial malleolus (distal 6 cm).
2. Bone tenderness at the base of the 5th metatarsal or the navicular bone.
3. Inability to bear weight for 4 steps both immediately after injury and during evaluation.

## Graded Rehabilitation
- **Grade I (Mild stretching):** Early weight-bearing as tolerated, ankle alphabet range-of-motion, single-leg balance within 3–5 days. Return to play in 1–2 weeks.
- **Grade II (Partial tear):** Functional semi-rigid ankle brace for 2–3 weeks, 4-way resistance band strengthening (eversion/dorsiflexion focus), wobble-board proprioception.
- **Grade III (Complete rupture):** Requires sports physician assessment, immobilization/boot phase followed by structured physiotherapy.

## Recurrence Prevention
Single-leg eyes-closed balance drills (3 x 45 seconds daily) and external lace-up ankle bracing reduce recurrent ankle sprain risk by up to 50%.
