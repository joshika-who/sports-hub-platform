# Dynamic Warm-Up Routines & Neuromuscular Activation

## Why Dynamic Warm-Ups Outperform Static Stretching Before Play
Modern sports medicine recommends a 10-to-15-minute structured dynamic warm-up before training or competition. Prolonged static stretching (>60 seconds) immediately before explosive sports can transiently reduce peak power output by 3–5%, whereas dynamic movement increases core muscle temperature, synovial fluid viscosity, and neuromuscular firing rate.

## The RAMP Protocol
1. **Raise (3–4 minutes):** Elevate heart rate and blood flow with light jogging, skipping, high knees, butt kicks, and lateral shuffles.
2. **Activate & Mobilize (4–5 minutes):**
   - World's Greatest Stretch (lunge with thoracic rotation) – 5 reps per side.
   - Glute bridges & monster walks with mini-band – 10 reps.
   - Walking leg swings (anterior-posterior and lateral) – 10 reps per leg.
   - Scapular push-ups and arm circles for overhead sports (tennis, badminton, basketball).
3. **Potentiate (3–4 minutes):**
   - Progressive build-up sprints at 60%, 75%, and 90% max effort.
   - Deceleration and plant-and-cut drills with soft knee landings.
   - Sport-specific reactive footwork.

## Post-Session Cool-Down
Reserve static holds (30–45 seconds per muscle group targeting calves, hamstrings, hip flexors, and pectorals) and foam rolling for the post-exercise cool-down to aid parasympathetic recovery.
