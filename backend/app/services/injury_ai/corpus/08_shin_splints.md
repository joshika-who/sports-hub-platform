# Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome (Shin Splints) vs. Tibial Stress Fracture

## Understanding Shin Splints
Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome (MTSS) is an exercise-induced overload injury along the posteromedial border of the distal two-thirds of the tibia, where the soleus, tibialis posterior, and flexor digitorum longus attach to the periosteum. It typically follows sudden spikes in running mileage (>10% weekly increase), hard court surfaces, or worn-out footwear.

## Critical Distinction: MTSS vs. Stress Fracture
- **Shin Splints (MTSS):** Diffuse tenderness spread over at least 5 cm along the posteromedial tibial border; pain often warms up during early exercise and aches afterward.
- **Tibial Stress Fracture (RED FLAG):** Focal pinpoint bone tenderness (<2 cm area), pain that worsens progressively throughout weight-bearing or walking, night pain, or pain on single-leg hop test. **Requires immediate cessation of impact activity and physician evaluation.**

## Management & Prevention Plan
1. **Acute Load Management:** Replace hard-surface running with zero-impact cross-training (swimming, deep-water running, cycling) for 10–14 days until walking and hopping are pain-free.
2. **Calf-Soleus Capacity Building:**
   - Bent-knee calf raises (targets the soleus, which absorbs up to 6–8x body weight during running) – 3 sets x 15 reps.
   - Straight-knee eccentric heel drops – 3 sets x 15 reps.
   - Tibialis anterior wall raises and towel toe-curls for intrinsic foot arch support.
3. **Cadence & Footwear:** Increasing running cadence by 5–7% reduces tibial peak impact loading. Replace court/running shoes every 500–650 km.
