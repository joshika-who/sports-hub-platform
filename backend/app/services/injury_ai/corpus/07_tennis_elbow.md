# Lateral Epicondylalgia (Tennis Elbow): Tendon Loading & Ergonomics

## Pathophysiology
Commonly known as "Tennis Elbow," lateral epicondylalgia is an overuse tendinopathy of the **Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB)** origin at the lateral epicondyle of the humerus. It is caused by repetitive wrist extension and forearm supination against impact forces (late backhand strokes, overly tight grip, or heavy racquet/stiff strings).

## Characteristic Signs
- Burning or aching pain 1–2 cm distal to the bony lateral epicondyle.
- Pain reproduced by resisted wrist extension, resisted middle-finger extension, or gripping objects (coffee mug, racquet handle).

## Evidence-Based Tendon Loading Protocol
Tendons respond best to progressive mechanical loading rather than complete rest:
1. **Isometric Analgesia (Days 1–10):** Hold a light dumbbell (1–3 kg) with forearm supported on a table, palm facing down, wrist extended 20° for 5 sets of 45 seconds (reduces cortical pain inhibition).
2. **Eccentric Tyler Twist / Wrist Extensions:** Slowly lower the weight into wrist flexion over 4 seconds using the injured arm, then use the healthy hand to lift it back up (3 sets x 15 reps daily).
3. **Forearm Pronation/Supination:** Slow controlled rotations using a hammer or weighted bar.

## Equipment & Technique Adjustments
- Check grip size (an overly small or large grip increases forearm flexor/extensor strain).
- Reduce string tension by 2–4 lbs or switch from stiff polyester to multifilament strings.
- Use a counterforce forearm strap 2–3 cm below the elbow crease during play to disperse tensile load.
