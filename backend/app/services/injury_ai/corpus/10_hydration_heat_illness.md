# Hydration Strategy, Electrolytes & Exertional Heat Illness Prevention

## Performance Impact of Dehydration
Fluid loss exceeding **2% of body mass** impairs thermoregulation, increases cardiovascular strain (heart rate drift), reduces cognitive reaction speed in court sports, and elevates muscle cramping and heat exhaustion risk.

## Structured Hydration Protocol for Athletes
1. **Pre-Exercise (2–4 hours prior):** Drink 5–7 mL of fluid per kg of body weight (approx. 400–600 mL for a 70 kg athlete) with a balanced meal or sodium source. Check urine color (aim for pale straw/lemonade color).
2. **During Exercise:**
   - Sessions <60 minutes: 150–250 mL of cool water every 15–20 minutes.
   - Sessions >60 minutes or high heat/humidity: Isotonic sports drink containing **6–8% carbohydrates** and **400–700 mg/L of sodium** to prevent exercise-associated hyponatremia and sustain glycogen oxidation.
3. **Post-Exercise Rehydration:** Weigh before and after training. Consume **1.25 to 1.5 Liters of fluid per 1.0 kg of body weight lost**, paired with sodium and potassium electrolytes.

## Recognizing Exertional Heat Illness
- **Heat Cramps:** Painful involuntary spasms in calves/hamstrings; move to shade, stretch gently, and replenish oral sodium-electrolyte fluids.
- **Heat Exhaustion:** Profuse sweating, core temp <40°C (104°F), dizziness, headache, tachycardia, nausea. Stop play immediately, remove excess gear, apply ice towels to neck/axilla/groin, elevate legs.
- **Exertional Heat Stroke (MEDICAL EMERGENCY):** Core temp >40°C (104°F) with central nervous system dysfunction (confusion, collapse, irrational behavior, ataxia). **Call emergency medical services immediately and initiate rapid whole-body cold-water immersion ("Cool first, transport second").**
