# Acute Soft-Tissue Injury Management: From RICE to PEACE & LOVE

## Overview
Historically, the **RICE** (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) protocol was standard for acute sprains and strains. Modern sports medicine (British Journal of Sports Medicine) has evolved to the **PEACE & LOVE** framework, balancing immediate protection with optimal tissue remodeling.

## Phase 1: Immediate Care (First 48–72 Hours) — PEACE
- **P – Protection:** Unload or restrict movement for 1–3 days to minimize bleeding and prevent distension of injured fibers. Avoid complete prolonged immobilization.
- **E – Elevation:** Elevate the injured limb higher than the heart as often as comfortable to promote interstitial fluid drainage.
- **A – Avoid Anti-Inflammatories:** Avoid high-dose NSAIDs (like ibuprofen) and excessive icing in the first 48 hours unless prescribed by a physician, as natural inflammation initiates tissue repair. Ice may still be used briefly (10 mins) purely for severe pain analgesia.
- **C – Compression:** Use an elastic bandage or kinesiology tape to limit intra-articular edema and tissue hemorrhage (ensure distal circulation is not compromised).
- **E – Education:** Understand that active recovery outperforms passive modalities.

## Phase 2: Sub-Acute Rehabilitation (After 72 Hours) — LOVE
- **L – Load:** Introduce pain-free mechanical stress early. Optimal loading stimulates collagen alignment and tendon/ligament tensile strength.
- **O – Optimism:** Psychological readiness improves rehabilitation adherence and outcomes.
- **V – Vascularisation:** Begin pain-free cardiovascular activity (stationary cycling, swimming, upper-body ergometer) to boost blood flow to healing tissues.
- **E – Exercise:** Progressively restore mobility, proprioception (balance), and eccentric strength.
